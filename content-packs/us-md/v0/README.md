# Maryland Content Pack (us-md v0)

## Jurisdiction-Specific Notes

### Wills (Estates & Trusts Article, §§ 4-101 et seq.)
- **Witnesses:** 2 attesting witnesses required (§ 4-102). Witnesses must sign in testator's presence.
- **Self-proving:** Maryland allows self-proving affidavits (§ 4-104).
- **Holographic wills:** NOT valid in Maryland (unlike Virginia). All wills must meet formal attestation requirements.
- **Interested witnesses:** A will is not invalid because a witness is interested, but the interested witness's bequest may be void unless there are 2 disinterested witnesses (§ 4-102(b)).

### Power of Attorney (Estates & Trusts Article, §§ 17-101 et seq.)
- Maryland adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act (2010).
- **Execution:** Signed by principal, acknowledged before notary. Witnesses recommended but not strictly required for validity.
- **Durability:** Presumed durable unless stated otherwise.

### Advance Directives (Health-General Article, §§ 5-601 et seq.)
- **Witnesses:** 2 witnesses required. At least 1 witness must not be a beneficiary or have a claim against the estate.
- Maryland recognizes: living wills, healthcare agent (durable POA for healthcare), and MOLST forms.

### Key Differences from Virginia
1. Holographic (handwritten) wills are NOT valid in MD — VA allows them.
2. MD has stricter interested-witness rules that can void bequests.
3. MD uses the Estates & Trusts Article; VA uses Title 64.2.
