# Pour-over wills

A pour-over will is a will designed to work alongside a revocable living
trust. Instead of distributing your assets to people directly, it leaves
whatever it controls to your trust: at your death, those assets "pour
over" into the trust and are distributed under its terms.

Why have one if you already have a trust?

- **Safety net.** Funding a trust is an ongoing task, and most people
  miss an asset somewhere — an account opened later, a refund check, a
  forgotten property interest. The pour-over will catches whatever was
  left out and sends it into the trust, so your plan's instructions
  still govern it.
- **Guardians.** Only a will can nominate a guardian for minor children;
  a trust cannot. Parents with a trust still need a will for this alone.
- **One set of instructions.** Because everything ends up in the trust,
  the distribution plan lives in a single document instead of being
  split between a will and a trust that might drift apart.

The trade-off: assets that pass through a pour-over will still go
through probate first — the will is what moves them. Assets already
titled in the trust during your lifetime skip probate. That is why the
pour-over will is a backup, not a substitute for funding the trust
while you are alive.

Virginia law expressly permits wills that leave assets to a trust,
including one you amend after signing the will. This guide is
educational only and is not legal advice.
