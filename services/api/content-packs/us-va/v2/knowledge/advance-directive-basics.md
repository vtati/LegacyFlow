# Advance medical directives

An advance medical directive is the document that speaks for you when
you cannot communicate your own medical decisions. Virginia's Health
Care Decisions Act governs these documents.

An advance directive typically does two things:

- **Names a health care agent** — the person who makes medical decisions
  for you when your care team determines you cannot make or communicate
  them yourself. Most people also name one or two backup agents.
- **States treatment wishes** — your instructions about life-sustaining
  treatment if you are terminally ill or in a persistent vegetative
  state, and other care preferences you want known.

Practical points:

- The document must be signed by you and witnessed by two adults.
- Your agent's authority generally begins when your attending physician
  determines that you lack the capacity to make your own decisions.
- A directive can cover organ donation wishes and preferences about
  pain management and other care, not only end-of-life treatment.
- Give copies to your agent, your backups, and your doctors, and make
  sure your family knows where the original is. A directive no one can
  find cannot help.
- You can change or revoke your directive at any time while you are
  able to communicate.

An advance directive is different from a financial power of attorney:
the directive covers health care, the POA covers money and property.
Many plans also include a HIPAA authorization so your agent can access
the medical information needed to make decisions.
