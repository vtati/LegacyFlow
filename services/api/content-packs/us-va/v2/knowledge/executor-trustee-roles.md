# Executors and trustees

Executors and trustees are the people who carry out your plan. Both are
fiduciaries — the law requires them to act in the beneficiaries'
interests, not their own — but their jobs differ.

**Executor** (called a personal representative in some contexts):

- Is named in your will and formally appointed by the court.
- Administers the estate through probate: gathers assets, pays debts
  and taxes, files the required inventories and accountings, and
  distributes what remains.
- Works under court and Commissioner of Accounts supervision, and the
  job usually lasts months to a couple of years.

**Trustee**:

- Manages the assets held in your trust, under the trust's own terms —
  usually without court involvement.
- May serve for many years if the trust holds assets for children or
  other beneficiaries over time.
- Keeps records, manages investments prudently, and makes the
  distributions the trust document describes.

Choosing well matters more than choosing impressively. Useful qualities
are trustworthiness, basic financial organization, willingness to ask
professionals for help, and the time to do the work. Many people name a
spouse or adult child first, with a successor behind them; others name a
bank or trust company, which charges fees but serves impartially and
does not age, move away, or become ill.

Always name successors. A plan whose only named executor or trustee
cannot serve falls back to court appointment — the outcome the plan was
built to avoid. It is also wise to tell the people you name, so the role
does not arrive as a surprise.
