# Beneficiaries and beneficiary designations

A beneficiary is a person or organization you name to receive an asset.
There are two different systems for naming them, and they do not talk to
each other:

- **Your will or trust** names beneficiaries for the assets it controls.
- **Beneficiary designations** are the forms held by financial
  institutions for specific accounts: life insurance, retirement
  accounts, annuities, and payable-on-death or transfer-on-death
  accounts.

The designation form wins. If your will leaves everything to your spouse
but an old insurance form names someone else, the insurance pays the
person on the form. Keeping designations current is one of the most
important — and most commonly missed — parts of a plan.

Terms you will see:

- **Primary beneficiary**: first in line to receive the asset.
- **Contingent beneficiary**: receives the asset only if the primary
  beneficiary cannot (for example, if they died before you).
- **Per stirpes**: a way of passing a deceased beneficiary's share down
  to their own children, rather than splitting it among the surviving
  beneficiaries.

Situations that call for a designation review: marriage, divorce, a
birth or adoption, a beneficiary's death, opening or rolling over an
account, and creating a trust (your attorney may recommend naming the
trust for some assets — retirement accounts need special care, because
naming a trust can change how the money is taxed).

Never name minor children directly on a designation if the plan intends
a trustee or guardian to manage the money for them; a direct designation
pays the money out without that protection. This guide is educational
only and is not legal advice.
