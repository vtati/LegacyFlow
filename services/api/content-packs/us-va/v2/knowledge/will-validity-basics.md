# Will validity basics (Virginia)

This is general educational information about what Virginia law requires for a
will to be valid. It is not legal advice, and your attorney finalizes whether
any particular document meets these requirements.

Under Virginia law, a valid will generally must:

- Be in writing. Oral statements of wishes are not a valid will.
- Be signed by the person making the will (the testator), or signed by
  someone else in the testator's presence and at the testator's direction.
- Be attested by two competent witnesses who sign in the presence of the
  testator.
- Be made by a person who is at least 18 years old and of sound mind.

Virginia also recognizes holographic wills: a will written entirely in the
testator's own handwriting and signed by the testator can be valid without
witnesses, as long as the handwriting and signature are proved.

A will that does not meet these requirements may be found invalid, which is
why an attorney reviews every will before it is finalized. This guide is
educational only and is not legal advice.
