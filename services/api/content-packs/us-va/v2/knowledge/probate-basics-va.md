# Probate in Virginia

Probate is the court-supervised process that validates a will, appoints
the person who will administer the estate, and oversees paying debts and
taxes and distributing what remains.

How it generally works in Virginia:

- **Where**: the will is offered for probate at the clerk's office of
  the circuit court in the city or county where the person lived.
  Virginia has no separate probate court.
- **Qualification**: the executor named in the will (or an administrator,
  if there is no will) formally qualifies before the clerk, takes an
  oath, and usually posts a bond — many wills waive the bond or its
  surety.
- **Oversight**: estates are supervised by a Commissioner of Accounts, a
  local official who reviews the estate's inventory and accountings. The
  executor files an inventory of estate assets, then periodic accounts
  showing money received and spent, ending with a final accounting.
- **Creditors and taxes**: debts, final taxes, and administration costs
  are paid from the estate before beneficiaries receive their shares.

Not everything goes through probate. Assets in a funded trust, assets
with beneficiary designations, jointly owned property with right of
survivorship, and payable-on-death accounts pass outside the process.

Virginia also has a shortcut for very small estates: when the estate's
assets are worth $50,000 or less, a small estate affidavit can be used
to collect assets after 60 days have passed, without a full
administration.

Probate timelines vary — months for a simple estate, longer when there
are disputes, property to sell, or tax complications. This guide is
educational only and is not legal advice.
