# North Carolina Content Pack (us-nc v0)

## Jurisdiction-Specific Notes

### Wills (N.C.G.S. Chapter 31)
- **Witnesses:** 2 attesting witnesses required (§ 31-3.3). Must sign in testator's presence.
- **Self-proving:** NC allows self-proving affidavits (§ 31-11.6).
- **Holographic wills:** Valid in NC if entirely in testator's handwriting and signed, proved by 3 witnesses to handwriting (§ 31-3.4). Stricter than VA (requires 3 vs. 2 witnesses).
- **Nuncupative (oral) wills:** Limited validity for personal property only, with strict requirements (§ 31-3.5).

### Power of Attorney (N.C.G.S. Chapter 32C — Uniform Power of Attorney Act)
- NC adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act (2018).
- **Execution:** Signed by principal, acknowledged before notary. Unlike VA, NC REQUIRES the POA to be registered with the county Register of Deeds to be effective for real property transactions.
- **Durability:** Presumed durable.

### Advance Directives (N.C.G.S. Chapter 90, Article 23)
- **Healthcare POA:** NC's primary form. Must be signed, witnessed by 2 qualified witnesses, and notarized.
- **Living Will:** Separate document under NC law (Declaration of Desire for Natural Death).
- **Witness requirements:** Witnesses cannot be related by blood/marriage, entitled to estate, or the attending physician.

### Key Differences from Virginia
1. NC holographic wills require 3 handwriting witnesses (VA requires 2).
2. NC POAs must be REGISTERED with county for real estate — VA has no registration requirement.
3. NC separates Healthcare POA and Living Will into distinct documents; VA combines them in the Advance Medical Directive.
4. NC witness disqualification is broader for advance directives.
