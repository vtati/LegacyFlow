Section 1 · Overview

What LegacyFlow is

LegacyFlow helps a family create a complete, attorney-approved estate plan — a Will, powers of attorney, healthcare wishes, guardian choices for children, and, if they want one, a living trust — without the family ever having to write a legal document or visit a law office to get started.

The problem it solves. Most families know they need an estate plan but stall: lawyers are expensive and intimidating, do-it-yourself websites produce documents nobody has checked, and the information a lawyer needs is scattered across the family's memory, filing cabinets, and bank accounts. LegacyFlow splits the work into the parts each side does best. The family answers plain-English questions at their own pace, with an AI assistant that interviews them conversationally. Software then turns those answers into draft legal documents, checks them for mistakes, and organises everything. A licensed attorney at a real law firm reviews the whole package, asks questions if anything is unclear, approves it, and only then are the final documents released for signing.

The three sides of the product.

🏠 Families

Sign up on their own, answer questions in an AI-guided interview or a step-by-step form, upload supporting documents, watch drafts being created, and receive attorney-approved final documents with a checklist for signing them correctly.

⚖️ Law firms & attorneys

Receive organised, pre-checked client files ("matters") in a workspace built for review: the family's answers, draft documents, automatic consistency findings, and a short series of approval steps that end with releasing clean final documents.

🛠️ The platform

LegacyFlow itself supplies the legal content for each state (the questions, the document templates, the safety rules, the signing requirements), connects each family to a law firm licensed in their state, and keeps the safety rails: nothing becomes a final legal document without a licensed attorney's approval.

Two promises that shape everything below. First, the AI never gives legal advice and never approves anything — it interviews, organises, drafts, and double-checks; the attorney decides. Second, draft documents are visibly watermarked "DRAFT — NOT LEGALLY VALID" until the attorney releases the finals. A plan only counts as finished once it has been signed correctly, and the product guides that too.

Where it works today. Virginia is the live state: a Virginia family can sign up today and be connected automatically to Virginia's designated law firm. Other states follow exactly the same model as their law firms and legal content are stood up (see Section 22 for the honest detail).

2 · Getting started

Everything a person needs to go from "never heard of LegacyFlow" to "my plan is underway."

1Create an account

FamiliesAttorneys

Sign-up asks for just an email address and a password (8–72 characters, entered twice). No name, no state, no payment, and no Google account is needed. If email confirmation is switched on, the first screen afterwards says "check your email" — the confirmation link signs the account into existence; otherwise the new user lands straight in their family home page.

Behind the scenes: accounts are handled by the sign-in service (Supabase Auth). Nothing about a plan or a household is decided yet — that happens when the first plan is started.

2Sign in — three ways

Everyone

Returning users can sign in with Google, with an email + password, or with a magic link — type your email and a sign-in link arrives that works for 15 minutes, no password needed. After signing in, you are returned to the page you were heading to (for example, an invitation or a specific plan).

3Forgot your password

Everyone

Enter your email and a reset link is sent, valid for one hour. The page deliberately never confirms whether an account exists for that email ("If an account exists, a reset link is on its way") — that vagueness is a safety feature against strangers probing for accounts. The link opens a set-a-new-password screen, and on success you are signed in and taken home automatically.

4Start a new estate plan

Families

From the home page, "Start new estate plan" opens a short form: an optional household name (it defaults to "My Household" — most families type something like "The Sharma Family"), an optional plan title, your state (Virginia, Maryland, District of Columbia, or North Carolina), and the kind of plan: Last Will and Testament, Revocable Living Trust, Power of Attorney, or the Complete Estate Plan Bundle.

Behind the scenes: the system loads that state's legal content pack (its questions, templates, and rules), finds the law firm registered as that state's default firm, and creates your household under that firm — there is no separate "find a lawyer" step. If a state has no default firm accepting plans yet, you are told plainly ("No law firm is currently accepting self-serve plans in this jurisdiction") instead of being left in limbo. You are then taken straight into your plan's interview.

5Joining through a law firm's invitation instead

FamiliesFirm teams

Not everyone starts self-serve. A law firm can invite a client by email; accepting the invitation creates the household (or joins you to one the firm prepared) and the plan simply appears in your account. Invitations expire after 7 days, can be resent by the firm, and can be revoked before they're used.

Behind the scenes: only a secure fingerprint of each invitation link is stored, never the link itself, and accepting is safe to click twice — it can never create a duplicate membership.

3 · Household & people

An estate plan is mostly about people — who your children are, who would raise them, who would act for you. LegacyFlow keeps one careful list of your people and reuses it everywhere, so a name is typed once and spelled the same way in every document.

6Your household profile

Families

The profile lists everyone in your household: name, how they're related to you, whether they have access to the account, email, and date of birth. Any person's details can be corrected here (name, date of birth, email, adult or minor).

7Adding people — dependents and trusted contacts

Families

You can add people in two roles: Dependent (typically your children) and Trusted contact (someone the firm may need to know about, without giving them your plan's controls). A dedicated Dependents page makes adding a child one small form — name, date of birth, optional email.

Behind the scenes: the two account-holder roles — the primary client (you) and a collaborator (such as a spouse who shares the work) — are created only when a household is set up, by invitation, or by the firm. That's deliberate: an estate plan must always know exactly whose plan it is, so the "add a person" form can never accidentally create a second plan owner. Only the primary client, a collaborator, or firm staff can manage the people list.

8Nominating guardians and other key people

Families

On the Guardians page you choose, in priority order, who fills each key role: guardian (raises your minor children), executor (carries out your Will), trustee (manages a trust), financial agent (acts under your power of attorney), and healthcare agent (makes medical decisions if you can't). Each nomination picks a person from your household list, a priority number (1 = first choice, 2 = backup, and so on), and optional notes. Nominations can be reordered by changing priority and can be removed.

Behind the scenes: nominations are stored per plan, in priority order, pointing at your people list — so if you correct a person's name once, every nomination (and every document) uses the corrected name. Answers you give in the interview and intake form create these nominations automatically; this page is where you can see and adjust them directly.

9Family & Guardians — the read-only overview

Families

A single screen that shows your household at a glance: adults and minors with their details, and every nomination across all five roles with its priority and status. It's the "is everyone in place?" view; edit links jump from here to the pages where changes are made.

4 · Your money picture

Good planning needs the full picture of what you own and owe. These pages are working lists for you and your attorney — they also power the follow-up tasks that make a trust actually work (Section 11).

10Assets — what you own

Families

Add each asset with its type (bank account, investment, retirement account, real estate, business interest, life insurance, vehicle, or other), a name, an estimated value, how it's owned (solely, jointly, in a trust, and so on), and notes. The page totals your assets as you go. Deleting an asset asks for confirmation and removes its beneficiary details with it.

11Beneficiaries per asset — and the alignment check

Families

Each asset can record who should receive it (its beneficiary designations, with shares). A "Check alignment" button on any asset re-runs a rules check comparing those designations with the rest of your plan and shows plain-language flags — for example, a designation that no longer matches your choices — or reassures you it "looks aligned." The point is to surface mismatches for your attorney before they become expensive surprises; many real-world estate disputes start with a forgotten beneficiary form that contradicts the Will.

12Liabilities — what you owe

Families

The mirror list: mortgages, loans, credit cards, and other debts, with current balance and optional monthly payment, totalled at the top. The page says plainly that it's fine to leave it empty if you're debt-free.

13Net position — assets & accounts roll-up

Families

A read-only summary per household: total assets, total liabilities, your net position, and breakdowns by type, with links back to the editing pages.

Worth knowing: these lists inform your attorney and drive your follow-up task list, but they are not pasted into your Will or trust as an asset schedule. The documents are built from your verified interview/intake answers — a separate "Assets and accounts" section of the intake asks the document-relevant questions in document wording.

5 · The AI Interview

The interview is a conversation with an AI assistant that interviews you the way a good intake specialist would: one topic at a time, in plain English, remembering everything you've already said. It sits alongside the guided form (Section 6) — both fill in the same answers, so you can switch between talking and typing whenever you like. The interview is switched on per law firm; if your firm hasn't enabled it, the page says so and points you to the guided form instead.

14The conversation itself

Families

The assistant asks one question at a time; you type (Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a new line), watch typing dots while it thinks, and answer in your own words — full sentences are fine. Answers can show their homework: an expandable "Sources" list quotes the legal-content passage each explanation came from. Some questions the assistant will politely decline and redirect to your attorney — anything that needs legal judgment rather than facts. You can Pause and Resume at any time; progress is saved as you go, and reopening the interview picks up your recent conversation. When everything is collected, the session shows as Completed.

Behind the scenes: the assistant works through your state's question sets topic by topic (household, guardians, assets, and so on). Where a firm's interview is on the newer "v2" setting, it gathers each topic's facts together as a short numbered list instead of strict one-by-one questions.

15"Verify values" — nothing is filed without your OK

Families

This is the interview's most important safety design. The assistant never writes facts into your file directly. Everything it picks up from what you say — a name, a date, a choice — appears as a card in the Verify values panel showing the field, the proposed value, a confidence percentage, and where it came from ("You said: '…'" or "From your document, page 3"). For each card you can Confirm it, Edit it and save your correction, or Reject it. Only confirmed or corrected values become part of your plan.

16Talk instead of type — dictation

Families

A microphone button records your voice in the browser and transcribes it into the message box for you to review first — it is never sent automatically, so you can fix anything the transcription misheard before it counts. If the microphone is denied or unavailable, a short message explains and typing works as normal.

17Upload a document — facts extracted for you

Families

Already have paperwork — an old Will, a deed, an insurance summary? Upload a PDF or Word document (up to 10 MB) from the interview and the system reads it and turns the facts it finds into Verify-values cards (Section 15), each labelled with the document and page it came from. Facts that duplicate something already waiting for review are skipped and reported, not doubled. The result is always explained in plain words: values proposed, or "no readable text found," or "this looks like a scanned image we can't read — please type the details instead." Past uploads and their outcomes stay listed in the panel.

18"Don't know yet" — parking a question

Families

Stuck on a question — a guardian's address, an account detail? Every unanswered item in the coverage checklist has a "Don't know yet" link that parks it. Parked questions are counted separately so your progress reflects real unknowns rather than blanks, and you can unpark with "Answer now" whenever you're ready. The page is upfront about the one rule: parked questions must be answered before your documents can be created.

19Progress & coverage checklist

Families

A progress panel shows how complete your interview is, how many required items are still needed (or "All required items are collected"), how many are parked, and a checklist with a status dot for every question — answered, saved for later, waiting for your verification, or not yet answered. On a phone, the chat is sized so the typing box always sits at the bottom of the screen, the conversation stays pinned to the newest message, and your place is kept even when the on-screen keyboard opens and closes.

20The consistency check

Families

A Run consistency check button compares all your verified facts against each other and raises questions for your attorney — for example, two facts that contradict each other, or something that looks incomplete. The panel says exactly what it is: "Compares the facts you have verified and raises questions for your attorney — never legal conclusions." A clean result says so ("No questions raised — your verified facts look consistent"). Anything raised is recorded for the attorney's review with a severity — some findings are routine ("review"), some flag a material tension ("attention").

Behind the scenes: findings become part of your file's escalation list (Section 13) and are worded only as questions. If the AI service is unavailable, the check is recorded as a partial, deterministic-only run — never dressed up as a full clean pass.

21"Guided answers" — ask the plan a question

FamiliesFirms

Both the family dashboard and the firm's matter view include an assistant panel: ask a plain question ("What is a healthcare agent?") and get an answer assembled from your state's approved educational content, with citations to the exact passages used. Questions that ask for legal advice or strategy are refused with a suggestion to ask your attorney — and the request is passed to the attorney as an escalation so nothing falls through the cracks. Questions no approved content covers are escalated the same way.

Behind the scenes: answers are built only from your plan's own content pack, from passages marked approved. Material still awaiting attorney approval ("sample" content) is excluded unless a deployment explicitly opts in — the assistant would rather escalate than improvise. Since November 2026 the passage search is hybrid: it matches by meaning as well as by exact words (chunk-level keyword + embedding retrieval over a pgvector index, chosen over the old keyword-only search on a measured golden question set — top-1 accuracy 0.77 vs 0.53), so a question phrased in everyday language finds the right guide even when it shares no words with it.

6 · The guided Intake form

The form version of the same interview: 21 short sections in a fixed order, each a handful of questions. (The AI Interview and this form write to the same answers — start in one, finish in the other.)

22The 21 sections

Families

In order: About your household · Quick check · Primary guardian · Backup guardians · What do you own? · Primary executor · Backup executor · Specific bequests · Residuary estate · Your address · Guardian address · Healthcare agent · Living will and organ donation · Your financial agent · Do you want a trust? · Trustee roles · Trust terms · Are your beneficiaries current? · Funeral and burial · Digital assets · Wishes for your children.

Behind the scenes: sections come from your state's question sets, and questions that don't apply to your family are quietly removed — a family with no minor children won't be asked guardian details, and the trust sections shrink if you decline a trust. Your progress, and the submission rules, count only the questions that actually apply to you.

23Question styles & "Why we ask"

Families

Questions render as the control that fits: yes/no and single-choice as radio buttons, multiple choices as checkboxes, "which person?" as a dropdown of your household, and free text, dates, and notes as fields. Required questions carry an asterisk. Where a question might seem intrusive or odd, a "Why we ask" disclosure under it expands to explain, in plain English, what the answer is used for.

24Autosave — you can't lose work

Families

Every change saves automatically about a second after you stop typing. A status line cycles through "Saving…", "All answers saved", and — if your connection drops — "Save failed — check your connection," so you always know where you stand. There is no Save button to forget.

25Progress that doesn't flatter you

Families

The header shows "X of Y required answered" and each section shows its own percentage — calculated honestly: percentages are rounded down, and a tick replaces the number only at a genuine 100%. Optional questions never inflate the score.

26Getting around — sidebar and the mobile tab strip

Families

On a computer, sections sit in a left-hand sidebar (number, title, percentage, mini progress bar). On a phone, the same list becomes a sticky horizontal tab strip pinned under the header: the chips scroll sideways inside the strip only (the page itself never scrolls sideways), each chip shows its section's progress, the current section's chip stays centred and highlighted, and the edges fade to hint there's more. Previous/Next buttons and a "Section X of Y" label sit at the foot of every section card.

27"Check my answers" — the rules evaluation

Families

Under every section, a Review-and-submit card offers Check my answers. It runs your state's safety rules over your answers. Routine notes appear as "Notes from your check"; a clean pass says "Your answers look complete"; and if a rule says your situation needs a lawyer's eyes before continuing — the panel says so directly ("Attorney review required") with the reason. Virginia's rules include things like: a child with special needs, property in another state, a prior marriage, expected family conflict, or wanting tax strategy — situations where a generic document would be irresponsible.

28Submitting from the form

Families

Submit for attorney review is available once three things are true: the plan is still in intake, every applicable required question is answered, and no blocking safety rule is open. If anything is missing, the form refuses politely and lists exactly what is still needed, by name — no detective work required. On success: "Submitted for attorney review — your answers are with your firm now." (Families whose firm has enabled self-serve drafts usually submit from the Review page instead — Section 7 — after seeing their documents.)

7 · Review, drafts & submitting

This is the family's quality gate: see every answer exactly as it will appear in your documents, fix anything, watch the drafts being made, then send the frozen package to your attorney.

29The Review page — every answer, in document wording

Families

All your answers are grouped by topic and written the way they'll read in the documents, each with inline edit-and-save. People and their roles appear here too, with names editable in place. A "Saved for later" card lists any parked questions with a link back into the interview — documents can't be created until each one is answered. Saving any edit automatically refreshes everything downstream: completeness, your nominations, and whether your drafts are still current.

30The automated pre-review (Attorney Reviewer)

FamiliesFirms

When your firm has this switched on, an "Automated pre-review" panel appears on the Review page. It runs automatically when drafts are created and again when you submit, and you can re-run it yourself. It checks your draft documents in two layers:

  • Rule checks (no AI): the same person's name spelled two ways across documents; someone named as both first choice and backup in the same role; dates and ages that don't add up (a birth date in the future, a "minor" who is 30); a document missing from your plan's set; blanks where you actually supplied the information; Virginia's signing formalities missing from the text (signature and witness blocks); and names mentioned in your free-text wishes that match nobody in your household — possible typos.
  • AI judgment pass: one careful read-through for contradictions between documents, implausible values, missing context, and ambiguity. It may only raise a point if it can quote the exact words from your draft that triggered it — anything it can't quote is thrown away, as is anything phrased like a legal conclusion.

Each finding is labelled "Worth fixing" or "Question for your attorney," grouped by document, with an explanation, the quoted excerpt, and a suggested fix. The panel states in plain words that this is an automated consistency check, not a legal review, and it never blocks your submission. If only the rule checks ran (for example, the AI service hiccupped), the panel says that too, rather than pretending a full review happened.

31Acknowledging a finding — "this one is correct as-is"

FamiliesFirms

Sometimes a "finding" is actually right — an unusual spelling that's genuinely the person's name, a beneficiary who really is outside the household. Either you or the firm can acknowledge such a finding, but only with a short written note explaining why it's OK; the button won't work without one. The record then shows who acknowledged it ("Acknowledged by the family" or by the firm), when, and the note. If the documents are regenerated and the identical finding reappears, the acknowledgment carries over automatically — you never have to justify the same thing twice.

32Create draft documents

Families

Once every applicable required answer is complete, Create draft documents unlocks (it also needs your firm to have document creation switched on, and your state's pack to support it). The plan's documents are generated from your answers by fixed legal templates — the wording is deterministic, never improvised by AI. A Virginia plan produces up to five documents:

DocumentWhat it doesWhen it's included
Last Will and TestamentSays who receives your property, names your executor, and names guardians for minor children.Always.
Power of AttorneyLets your chosen financial agent act for you (banking, bills, property) if you can't.Unless you chose not to create one.
Advance Medical DirectiveYour healthcare wishes (a "living will") plus your chosen healthcare agent, and your organ-donation wishes.Unless you chose not to create one.
Guardianship DesignationFormally names who would raise your minor children.Only if you have minor children.

Documents that don't apply stay visible in the list with their reason ("Only needed when you have minor children," "You chose not to create a power of attorney") so the set never looks accidentally incomplete. If a needed template is missing from the firm's library, generation stops entirely rather than producing a partial set.

If your firm hasn't enabled self-serve drafts: the panel says so and explains your firm will prepare your documents for you; your answers remain fully reviewable and editable, and you submit them from the intake form instead (Section 28).

33"Drafts out of date" — the freshness fingerprint

Families

Every set of drafts remembers exactly what it was built from — a fingerprint of the answers, people, and roles that feed the documents, plus the template version used. Change anything relevant afterwards and the Review page warns that your drafts are out of date and must be regenerated before submitting. This guarantees the attorney reviews precisely the documents that match your final answers — never a stale mixture.

34Submit to attorney — the freeze

Families

Submit to attorney checks, in order: not already submitted, the plan is in a submittable state, intake is complete, drafts exist, and drafts are current. Then three things happen at once:

  • Your plan is assigned to an attorney at your firm who is licensed in your state (the system picks the firm's approved attorney for your jurisdiction; if none is available, the submission stops with an explanation rather than sending your file nowhere).
  • A review packet — a frozen, numbered snapshot of your answers, people, nominations, choices, and any flagged items — is created for the firm (Section 13).
  • Your drafts are frozen as submitted. You keep full viewing access to your answers and documents, but you can no longer change the submitted set yourself — if something needs to change, your attorney asks, via a clarification (Section 13).

The page then shows "Submitted for attorney review," your assigned attorney's name, and the packet version.

8 · Your documents

35The Documents page — every version, kept

FamiliesFirms

Every document ever generated for your plan is listed with its type, version number, and date. Documents are never overwritten: each regeneration adds new, numbered versions, so the full history — what changed and when — is preserved. Each document exists as both a Word file (DOCX) and a PDF: PDFs open as a preview in a new browser tab, Word files download.

36The draft watermark

Families

Until release, everything carries the diagonal watermark and footer "DRAFT — NOT LEGALLY VALID," and the Documents page itself shows a red banner: "DRAFT — PENDING ATTORNEY REVIEW — these are watermarked drafts for review only; nothing here is final until your attorney approves it." Drafts are for reading and checking — they have no legal effect, and the product never lets that be ambiguous.

37Final documents after release

Families

When your attorney approves the release gate and releases production (Section 13), clean, watermark-free final versions are generated and appear in the same Documents list, and your home page's next step changes to point you at them and at signing.

One honest wrinkle: the family Documents page lists finals in the same list as drafts and currently labels every row "Draft · vN" — there is no separate "Finals" section yet. Your plan's status (on Home and My Plan) is the reliable signal that finals have been released; the documents themselves are the clean, unwatermarked files.

38Requesting a change after release — amendments

FamiliesFirms

Life changes: a new child, a new house, a different executor. Once your plan has been released, you (or the firm) can file an amendment request describing the change in plain language and, optionally, which documents it affects. Identical requests sent twice are de-duplicated. The request is decided by an attorney or firm admin (approve/reject, with notes, all recorded), and an approved change is carried out as a formal amendment document — a codicil for a Will or a trust amendment for a trust — rather than by quietly editing the signed originals.

9 · Files & the Vault

Two related features with confusingly similar names — worth separating: Files is where documents live; the Vault is how you share a summary of your plan with someone you trust.

39Files — your document storage

FamiliesFirms

Upload supporting documents to a plan — PDF, Word, JPEG, PNG, or WebP, up to 25 MB each. Every file shows its size and upload time and runs through a checking pipeline before it's available: its badge reads Scan pending, then Clean, or Quarantined if the file's contents don't match what it claims to be (the check reads the file's actual type from its bytes, not its name — a renamed file can't sneak through). Quarantined files can never be downloaded. Downloads use short-lived secure links (15 minutes). Only the account's primary client or a collaborator can upload; dependents and trusted contacts can view what they're given access to.

40AI analysis of an uploaded file

Families

Once a file's text has been extracted, an Analyze button offers an AI read of it: a classification (Will, Trust, Deed, Life insurance, Financial statement, Power of attorney, Advance directive, Tax return…), a plain summary paragraph, the key facts pulled out as label-and-value pairs, and suggested tags (merged with any tags you add yourself). It's how an old paper Will or a jumble of statements becomes something your attorney can scan in seconds. Two honest limits: photos/images can't be analysed this way, and a scanned PDF with no readable text can't either — the product tells you to try a text-based file instead of pretending.

41Vault shares — a safe link for someone you trust

Families

The Vault page creates share links for your plan. Give the link a label ("For my sister") and an optional expiry date, and you get a link that is shown exactly once — save it then; the list afterwards shows only the label, dates, and whether it's Active or Revoked. Revoking (with a confirmation) kills a link immediately.

Whoever opens the link — no sign-in needed — sees a deliberately redacted summary: the plan title, the label you gave it, the state, a list of your documents by filename and type, and the expiry date. Never the documents themselves, never your answers. It's designed for the "so my family knows a plan exists and where things stand" conversation. An expired, revoked, or mistyped link gets a neutral "This link doesn't work anymore" page that reveals nothing.

10 · Signing your plan

A Will that isn't signed correctly may not count. LegacyFlow doesn't do electronic signing — instead it prepares you, precisely, for the physical signing your attorney will walk you through.

42The signing checklist

FamiliesFirms

Once all your plan's documents are released, the firm generates your signing checklist from your state's execution rules, and your Signing page shows it: how many items you've reviewed ("X of Y reviewed"), the two headline facts — witnesses required (for a Virginia Will: two) and notary (for a Virginia Will: not legally required) — and each step in order. For a Virginia Will: the document must be in writing; you sign it; two competent witnesses sign in your presence and each other's; and a recommended (not required) extra — a notarised "self-proving affidavit" that can make the later court process simpler.

43"Mark reviewed" — preparation tracking, not a legal act

Families

Each checklist item asks you to tick an acknowledgement — "I have read this requirement and understand I must follow my attorney's instructions for it" — and mark it reviewed; the system stamps the time itself. The page is explicit about what this is and isn't: "Checking this box is not a legal certification — it only marks the requirement reviewed in this app. Your attorney confirms proper execution." The firm sees the same checklist with per-item attested/pending status, so they know you're ready before the signing appointment. There is no e-signature, no witness identity capture, and no uploading of signed copies on this page.

11 · Staying on track

44Home — "Your next step"

Families

Your home page always answers "what should I do next?" with one card driven by your plan's real status: finish your intake; answer your firm's questions (if they asked a clarification); review your released documents; go to signing; do your annual checkup — whatever is actually current. Around it: a greeting with your household name, your main plan's progress ring, counts of your plans, and cards for each plan with its status in plain words ("Intake in progress," "Attorney review," "Documents released," "Plan implemented") and links to continue.

45My Plan — the whole plan on one page

Families

For each plan: how complete it is ("X of Y required intake answers complete"), your key roles at a glance (guardian, executor, trustee — first choice plus how many backups, or "Not named yet"), up to ten of your choices so far in question-and-answer form, and dynamic next steps that point at whatever is missing. A banner at the top warns if any plan is missing a key person, or confirms when every plan has all three. A standing reminder heads the page: "Nothing here is final until your attorney approves it."

46Checkup — everything waiting on you, in one list

Families

Checkup gathers, per plan, anything that needs you: unanswered required questions; approval gates waiting on your firm; open questions your firm has asked you; signing items you haven't reviewed; funding tasks left incomplete; and key roles (guardian/executor/trustee) not yet named. Each item links straight to the page that resolves it, and the page says "All clear" when there's genuinely nothing. If some information couldn't be loaded, it warns that the list may be incomplete rather than showing a false "all clear."

47Funding tasks — making the plan actually work

FamiliesFirms

A trust only controls assets that are moved into it — "funding" is that follow-through: retitling accounts, updating beneficiary forms, reviewing deeds. The firm generates a personalised task list for your household from the state's funding playbooks matched against your assets (for example: review this bank account's transfer-on-death setup; confirm that retirement account's beneficiaries). Your Funding page lists each task with a status you control — Pending → In progress → Complete — and lets you attach proof (a statement, deed, or confirmation letter; same file rules as Section 39). A counter shows "X of Y tasks complete." Until the firm generates tasks, the page explains they'll appear here.

48Help — answers without messaging

Families

The Help page carries six frequently asked questions (intake, attorney review, why documents are watermarked, signing, funding, the Vault) and a small plain-English glossary. It is also honest about its limit: there is no in-app messaging — for anything urgent or sensitive you contact your firm directly using the phone and email on your engagement paperwork. Questions your attorney asks you inside the product arrive as clarifications, surfaced on Checkup and Home — not here.

49Notifications — what actually reaches you today

Everyone

There is no notification inbox or bell in the app. Sign-in emails (account confirmation, magic links, password resets) are sent by the sign-in service as you'd expect. Inside the product, the signals are the Home next-step card, the Checkup list, and on-screen banners (for example, "drafts out of date"). The system also prepares notification emails for events like invitations, submissions, attorney questions, gate decisions, and reminders, and firm users are notified inside their workspace when a family submits. Current-deployment honesty: the app's own outgoing notification emails are presently recorded in a test outbox rather than delivered to real inboxes, so firms contact families through their normal channels in the meantime. A reminder engine also exists — reminders can be scheduled for a person and the system checks every 15 minutes for due ones, respecting each person's email/reminder preferences and unsubscribe — but schedules are not yet created automatically anywhere in the product, so treat reminders as infrastructure that's ready, not a feature you'll see firing today.

Everything below is the law firm's side of the same product. Firm members hold one of four roles per firm: firm admin (runs the firm), attorney (reviews and approves), staff (prepares and manages), and auditor (a limited role that, in the current product, has almost no access to client matters — see Section 22). A person can belong to several firms; most screens work with their longest-standing active firm, while their payout history combines all firms.

50Firm overview — the dashboard

Firms

Six live counts computed from the firm's matters (total; awaiting review; in intake; in signing & funding; documents released; implemented), a "Needs attention" list — matters escalated, waiting on a clarification answer, or carrying open flags, each badged with its reason — and the five most recently updated matters. Every number and row clicks through to the matter itself.

51The matters list

Firms

All the firm's client plans in one searchable table: search by client, household, or title; filter by stage (intake, attorney review, approved, released, signing, funding, implemented, closed) or by jurisdiction; twenty per page. Columns show the household, jurisdiction, a plain status badge, and when the matter last changed.

The status journey (behind every badge): Draft → Intake in progress → Intake submitted → Attorney review (with side-trips to Clarification requested, and Escalated if a blocking rule fires) → Approved for assembly → Documents released → Signing in progress → Funding in progress → Plan implemented. A completed plan can return to Attorney review for its annual checkup or a life-event update, and almost any stage can be Archived (closed for good). The system refuses impossible jumps — a matter can't skip from intake to released — and records every change.

52The matter page — ten tabs

Firms

One client's whole file: Overview (summary, completeness, key people) · Files (the family's uploads) · Documents (packages, drafts, finals, amendment requests) · Signing (checklist progress) · Funding (task list) · Guided answers (the same citation-backed assistant the family has, scoped to this matter) · Review packet (the frozen snapshot + pre-review findings) · Comments (firm staff and the household can comment; every comment is part of the file) · Clarifications (questions to the family) · Approval gates. The header always shows the plan title, household, jurisdiction, status, and the attorney-assignment control.

53Assigning an attorney

Firms

Three ways an attorney ends up on a matter. Manually: any staff member picks from the firm's active attorneys in the matter header (or sets it back to Unassigned). Automatically: when a self-serve family submits, the system assigns the firm's approved attorney licensed in that matter's state — and refuses the submission if there isn't one (Section 34). By request: attorneys can raise their hand for unassigned work (Section 56), but a request never assigns by itself — a person at the firm still makes the assignment.

54The review queue

Firms

A read-only worklist of every matter that is anywhere in the intake-to-review stretch: its status, how many approval gates are pending, how many clarifications are open, and the latest packet version and date (or "No packet yet"). It's the morning triage screen; clicking a row opens the matter, where the actual work happens.

55Clarifications — asking the family a question

FirmsFamilies

When something in a file is unclear, an attorney or firm admin creates a clarification: a written question attached to the matter. Asking one moves the matter to "Clarification requested," surfaces on the family's Home and Checkup pages, and the household answers in the product; the exchange is stored on the matter for good. It's the product's structured alternative to email threads — the question and answer stay with the file they belong to.

56Clients page

Firms

A household-centred view derived from the firm's matters: each client household with its plans and statuses, linking into each matter. The invitation manager also lives here (Section 64) — inviting clients and team members is one system.

57Reports & CSV export

Firms

A simple operational report computed live from the firm's matters: totals (matters, average intake completeness, open clarifications, pending gates), a pipeline funnel showing how many matters sit at each lifecycle stage, a matters-by-state table, and a one-click CSV export of the whole picture. If some figures couldn't be loaded, the page warns that those totals are partial. (There is no financial or per-attorney performance reporting in the product today.)

13 · Attorney review & release

The heart of the firm's work: a frozen packet, an AI pre-review that helps but never decides, exactly two approval gates, and a release step that is deliberately hard to do by accident.

58The review packet — the frozen snapshot

Firms

The packet is a versioned, unchangeable snapshot of a matter at a moment in time: the plan's basics and completeness, all intake answers, household people and relationships, nominations (every guardian/executor/agent appointment with its priority), the family's recorded decisions, and every escalation flag. (It deliberately does not include the assets/liabilities lists.) A new packet is created automatically when a self-serve family submits, and any staff member can generate a fresh one at any time from the matter's Review packet tab; older versions remain viewable, newest first. Families never see the packet itself — it's firm work product; they see the pre-review findings (Section 30) on their own Review page.

59Escalations — the "attorney, look at this" list

FirmsFamilies (their own)

An escalation is a recorded flag that something in a family's situation deserves attorney attention. They're created automatically, never by hand, from three sources: the state's safety rules run over the family's answers (Virginia's include a special-needs child, out-of-state property, a prior marriage, expected family conflict, a business interest, and more); the consistency check (Section 20); and the AI assistant when someone asks it for legal advice or something outside the approved content. Severities run from info (noted only) through review and attention, up to blocking — a blocking flag moves the matter to "Escalated" and pauses progress until an attorney deals with the situation through the review workflow. For privacy, an escalation stores only the rule and the triage facts — never the family's free-text answers. The firm's Admin → Support page rolls all open escalations across matters into one list. (Known limitation: escalations can be filtered as open/resolved/dismissed, but the current product has no button that closes one — disposition happens through the matter's review workflow, and the flags remain visible as the record of what was raised.)

60The two approval gates

AttorneysFirm admins

Progress past review is controlled by exactly two gates, in order. Gate 1 — Intake approval: created and decided while the matter is in Attorney review; approving it moves the matter to "Approved for assembly." Gate 2 — Document release: decided while Approved for assembly; approving it moves the matter to "Documents released" and unlocks final-document production. Any staff member can create a gate, but only an attorney or firm admin can decide one. A rejection leaves the matter where it is (a note is asked for in the interface), a fresh gate can be created later, and only one gate of each type can be pending at a time. Every decision notifies the firm and the family's account holders and is written into the audit trail.

61The Review Decision Panel — where the buttons live

Firms

At the top of each matter, a decision panel offers exactly the actions that fit its status. In Attorney review: Approve (creates the intake gate if needed and approves it), Request clarification (asks the family a question, Section 55), and Escalate. In Approved for assembly: the single Approve document release action. The Attorney Reviewer findings (Section 30) sit in the packet tab of the same page — visible while deciding, but, by design, no finding can block or influence a gate; the attorney's judgment is the gate.

62Releasing the final documents

AttorneysFirm admins

"Release for production" is guarded four ways: the person must be an attorney or firm admin; the document-release gate must already be approved; intake must be complete; and document creation must be switched on. If any condition fails, the release refuses with the reason — it cannot be forced. A successful release re-renders every document clean: no watermark, in Word and PDF, marks the package released, and records the act in the audit trail. There is no un-release. Drafts remain drafts forever; finals are new versions, so the before/after is always preserved.

63The notary package

Firms

Once all of a matter's document packages have been released, the firm can download one combined PDF: the final documents plus the state's execution checklist — the bundle the attorney takes into the signing appointment.

64The attorney's own workspace

Attorneys

Attorneys also get a personal area. Dashboard: an availability toggle ("Accept new cases" / paused — it controls their profile status; note it doesn't change how the routing in Section 53 picks attorneys today), four earnings figures (total earned, pending, approved, paid out), their assigned cases, and their payment history. Opportunities: unassigned matters waiting in review, shown anonymised — state and status only, no client names until assigned — with the standard fee shown ($150 standard / $300 complex) and a "Request case" button. The page says plainly that requesting doesn't claim the case: the firm reviews requests, and assignment remains a separate, deliberate step.

65Attorney payments (payouts)

FirmsAttorneys

When one of an attorney's matters reaches "Plan implemented," a completion fee is created automatically — exactly one per matter: $150 standard, $300 for trust-based or bundled plans. The firm's queue moves it through pending → approved → paid (or disputed, with a reason; paid fees can't be disputed), and "Mark paid" records an optional bank-transfer reference. To be precise about what this is: the money itself moves by ordinary bank transfer outside the product — LegacyFlow tracks who is owed what and its status, it does not yet process payments. (Plumbing for automated payments via Stripe exists in the codebase but is not connected to anything; family pricing is likewise not charged through the product today.)

Firms

73Content pack management (platform team)

Platform admins

Two safety rules are built in: an imported pack always lands as a draft no matter what its paperwork claims, and a pack can't be scheduled to take effect more than a year out. Every import and status change is audit-logged, and approval records who approved it and when. New plans automatically use the applicable pack with the most recent effective date; plans already started keep the pack version they began with, so the rules can never change mid-plan underneath a family.

16 · Privacy & safety

77Who can see what

Everyone

The boundaries are simple to state:

  • Your household sees your plans, answers, documents, and files. Within the household, only the primary client and collaborators can change things or upload; dependents and trusted contacts have view-only access.
  • Your law firm — its admins, attorneys, and staff — sees the matters routed to it, including answers, files, and drafts, because reviewing them is the job. One role, auditor, currently has effectively no access to client matters.
  • Other firms and other families see nothing of yours. Unassigned work shown to attorneys is anonymised (state and status only).
  • Other platform firms' data never leaks through the audit trail either — each firm admin sees only their own firm's events.

Standing over everything: the AI can interview, draft, and flag — but only a licensed attorney at your firm can approve gates and release final documents; every generated document is watermarked until that moment; and every authenticated page carries the footer "Attorney review pending. Not legal advice."

78The audit trail

Firm admins

Every significant action in the product writes a permanent record — around 75 kinds of events: plans created, submitted, and moved between stages; answers saved; interview sessions and parked questions; people and nominations changed; checks evaluated and flags raised; packets generated; gates decided; documents generated, released, amended; templates and packs changed; files uploaded, scanned, analysed, and downloaded; shares created and revoked; invitations sent and accepted; flags switched; payouts created, approved, and paid; applications decided. Each entry records who (or what — person, system, or background worker) acted, on which firm and matter, what changed, and a correlation ID. It is viewable read-only by the firm's admin (Section 75) and cannot be edited from anywhere in the product.

79Download your data

Everyone

Any user can retrieve a copy of their own data — profile, households and memberships, plans, people, decisions, and assets — as a structured file from the service. Other people's private contact details are deliberately left out, and very large collections are capped with a clear "truncated" marker rather than failing silently.

80Request deletion

Everyone

A user can file a data-deletion request (with optional notes) and see their own requests' status. To be precise: the product takes and tracks the request; carrying it out is a deliberate, manual, out-of-band process by the platform team — there is no self-serve "delete everything now" button, by design, because estate files carry legal retention obligations that a human must weigh.

81Email & reminder preferences

Everyone

Each user has email and reminder preferences (both on by default) plus an unsubscribe that the reminder system honours — a person who has unsubscribed or switched reminders off is skipped automatically when reminders are dispatched.

82File safety

Everyone

Uploads are treated as untrusted until proven otherwise: type checked from the file's actual bytes, held as "pending" until the check passes, quarantined on any mismatch, downloadable only when clean and only through a 15-minute signed link, and served from dedicated document storage. (Precision for the technically curious reader: the current check verifies that a file is genuinely the type it claims to be; it is a format check, not an antivirus scan.)

17 · The family journey, step by step

Meet a composite Virginia family — married, two young children, a house, no existing plan. Here is their whole trip.

They hear about LegacyFlow and sign up

From the website they create an account with an email and password — under a minute, nothing else asked. System: the account is created with the sign-in service; a confirmation email may be sent; they land on an empty family home page.

They start a plan and pick Virginia

"Start new estate plan": they name the household "The Sharma Family," choose Virginia, and pick the Complete Estate Plan Bundle. System: loads Virginia's legal content pack, finds Virginia's registered default firm, creates their household under that firm, and opens their plan in the interview.

The AI interview

Over a couple of evenings — pausing whenever life intervenes — they chat with the assistant: who's in the family, who'd raise the kids, what they own, healthcare wishes. They dictate some answers by voice, upload their old (pre-children) Will, and watch facts from it appear as cards to confirm or correct. One question — a guardian's address — they park with "Don't know yet." System: nothing enters their file until they confirm it; parked questions are tracked separately; each answered item fills the same answer set the form uses.

They tidy up in the guided form

Preferring checkboxes for the remaining bits, they switch to the Intake form. The tab strip on their phone keeps all 21 sections one tap away; percentages tick up honestly; "Why we ask" explains a question about prior marriages. They unpark the guardian's address and finish. System: autosaves every change; progress counts only applicable, required answers — theirs reaches a genuine 100%.

The consistency check

They run the consistency check. It raises one question for the attorney: a savings account mentioned in the interview doesn't appear in their asset answers. They note it mentally — it's exactly the sort of thing the check exists for — and carry on. System: the finding is recorded on their file as an escalation, worded as a question, for the firm to see at review.

Reviewing every answer

On the Review page they read each answer in document wording and fix a misspelled middle name with inline edit. System: the edit refreshes their completeness and their nominations automatically.

Drafts are created

They click "Create draft documents" and get four: Will, Power of Attorney, Advance Medical Directive, and Guardianship Designation. The trust line explains it wasn't chosen, so no trust document appears. They open the Will's PDF preview — clearly watermarked "DRAFT — NOT LEGALLY VALID." System: documents are rendered from fixed Virginia templates and the automated pre-review runs immediately.

The pre-review speaks — and they answer it

The pre-review flags that the backup guardian's surname is spelled two ways across documents. They're sure of the right spelling, fix the wrong one, and regenerate. One remaining finding — a beneficiary who is a close friend, not a household member — really is intended, so they acknowledge it with a note saying so. System: regeneration re-runs the pre-review; the acknowledged finding is recorded with their note and won't need re-justifying if it reappears.

They submit

"Submit to attorney." The page confirms: submitted, packet version 1, and names their assigned attorney at the Virginia firm. Their drafts are now frozen — viewable, but no longer editable by them. System: verifies the drafts match the final answers (the fingerprint), assigns the firm's Virginia-licensed attorney, freezes the review packet, and moves the plan to Attorney review.

A question comes back — and they answer it

The attorney asks a clarification about the savings account the consistency check spotted. It appears on their Home page and Checkup list; they answer in the product. System: the exchange is stored on their matter; the plan returns to Attorney review.

Approval and release

Behind the scenes (next section), the attorney approves both gates and releases production. The family's home page updates: documents released — review your final documents. System: clean, unwatermarked Word and PDF finals are generated as new versions; the drafts remain in history.

Signing day

Their Signing page shows the checklist: written document, their signatures, two witnesses signing together, optional self-proving affidavit. They read each item, tick the acknowledgements, and mark them reviewed; the attorney then walks them through the actual signing. System: records each reviewed item with a timestamp; the firm watches the same checklist fill in.

Funding and finish

Funding tasks appear — confirm this account's beneficiaries, review that deed — and they work through them, attaching a confirmation letter as proof. Their plan reaches "Plan implemented," and they create a Vault share link labelled "For my sister" so she knows the plan exists and what it contains at a glance. System: task statuses and evidence are stored on the matter; the share link shows the redacted summary only, revocable any time.

21 · Glossary — plain-English definitions

Estate plan
The set of legal documents and choices that say what happens to your money, property, and children if you die or can't act for yourself.
Will (Last Will and Testament)
The document that distributes your property, names your executor, and can name guardians for minor children. It takes effect at death and usually goes through probate (court supervision).
Executor
The person you name to carry out your Will — gather assets, pay debts, and distribute what's left.
Power of Attorney (POA)
A document letting a person you choose (your financial agent) handle money and property matters for you, typically if you're incapacitated.
Advance Medical Directive
Your written healthcare wishes — including a "living will" for end-of-life care — plus the healthcare agent you name to decide when you can't. Virginia's combined form.
Healthcare agent
The person authorised to make medical decisions for you when you cannot make or communicate them yourself.
Guardian / Guardianship Designation
The person who would raise your minor children if you couldn't; the designation is the document naming them, in priority order.
Revocable Living Trust
A trust you create and control while alive; assets placed ("funded") into it pass to your beneficiaries on your terms, usually without probate. "Revocable" means you can change or end it.
Trustee
The person who manages trust assets according to the trust's terms — often you, at first, then a successor you name.
Beneficiary
Anyone who receives something under your plan — from the Will, the trust, or a specific asset's beneficiary form.
Funding (a trust)
The follow-through work of moving assets into the trust's name or updating beneficiary forms so the plan actually controls them.
Matter
The product's word for one client's case or plan file at a firm — everything about one plan lives in its matter.
Packet
The frozen, numbered snapshot of a family's answers, people, choices, and flags that the firm reviews — it can never be edited after the fact.
Gate
One of the two formal attorney approvals (intake approval, then document release) a matter must pass before finals can be produced.
Escalation
An automatically created flag that something in a family's situation needs an attorney's attention — from a safety rule, the consistency check, or the AI assistant's limits.
Default firm
The law firm registered to receive a state's self-serve families — in Virginia today, the Virginia Default Firm.
Jurisdiction / content pack
"Jurisdiction" is the state whose law governs the plan. Its content pack is the bundle of that state's questions, templates, rules, and guidance inside the product.
Codicil / Trust amendment
Formal documents that change part of an already-signed Will or trust without replacing the whole thing.
Self-proving affidavit
An optional notarised statement signed with a Will that can spare witnesses from testifying later, simplifying probate.

22 · What's live, what's not — the honest box

✅ Live today

  • Virginia, end to end: open self-serve signup, automatic routing to Virginia's default firm, the AI interview and guided intake, consistency check, draft generation, attorney review with both gates, clean final release, signing checklist, and funding tasks.
  • The full firm workspace: matters, packets, clarifications, comments, reports, templates, invitations, applications review, payouts tracking.
  • Attorney enrolment by application with manual verification, per firm.

🔀 Real, but switched on per firm

  • Document assembly — off until a firm is deliberately enabled (and its state's pack supports it). Without it, the firm prepares documents for the family instead of the family generating drafts.
  • The Attorney Reviewer pre-review — off by default per firm; where off, its panels simply don't exist.
  • The AI Interviewer — on by default but a firm can be without it; the guided form always works.
  • Interview v2 (the topic-cluster conversation style) — enabled per firm by the platform team.
  • Maryland, DC, and North Carolina appear in the state picker, but their content packs are not yet complete and no default firms are registered for them — today, only Virginia completes the journey. A family choosing another state is told no firm is accepting plans there yet.

🚧 Present in the building, not yet a working feature

  • Reminders: the scheduling and dispatch machinery exists and respects preferences, but nothing in the product creates reminder schedules yet.
  • The app's own notification emails (invitations, "your attorney asked a question," gate decisions) are generated and recorded in a test outbox in the current setup rather than delivered; sign-in emails (confirmation, magic link, password reset) do work, and firms use their normal channels meanwhile.
  • Payments: attorney fees are tracked in the product but paid by bank transfer outside it; automated payment plumbing exists in code, unwired. Families are not charged through the product.
  • Two feature switches ("AI assistance," "Funding reminders") are listed in settings but not yet connected to behaviour.
  • Smaller known gaps: the family Documents page labels released finals as "Draft" rows (Section 37); escalations have no close-out button (Section 59); amendment decisions exist in the service but have no approve/reject buttons in the firm interface yet; the team roster is view-only; the auditor role currently has effectively no access to client matters; one template-creation form suggests type names the service won't accept (the firm Templates page lists the correct set).

🚫 Intentionally not part of the product

  • Legal advice from AI. The AI interviews, organises, drafts from fixed templates, and flags inconsistencies. It refuses advice questions and passes them to the attorney. Legal judgment belongs to the licensed attorney — by design, permanently.
  • Final documents without an attorney. There is no path — for any user, at any setting — by which a watermark-free document is produced without the document-release gate's attorney approval.
  • Electronic signing. Signing is prepared and tracked, not performed, in the product; execution happens with the attorney, on paper, as state law expects.
  • Self-serve law firms. Firms are created only by the platform; attorneys join only by verified application.
  • In-app messaging. Families and firms communicate through structured clarifications inside the file, and their own channels outside it — there is deliberately no chat inbox to lose things in.
